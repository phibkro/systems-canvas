# Enterprise Model

**How is a purposeful undertaking organized, operated and regulated?**

An Enterprise Model represents the capabilities, processes, resources, governance, information and feedback needed to understand an undertaking. Here, *enterprise* includes public services, institutions, civil-society initiatives, companies, cooperatives, and interorganizational or human–AI collaborations—not just businesses.

## Minimum model

| Construct | Investigate |
|---|---|
| Boundary & environment | What undertaking is being examined? Which actors and conditions lie outside it? |
| Agents & stakeholders | Who acts, delegates, governs and bears consequences? These roles can overlap. |
| Capacity | Which resources, skills, tools, dependencies and buffers enable activity? |
| Operations | Which activities, processes, decisions and flows transform capacity into outcomes? |
| Outcomes | Who receives the benefits, harms and externalities? Over what time horizon? |
| Purpose & governance | Which goals, constraints, rights, obligations and appeals direct the work? |
| Observation & feedback | What is monitored, with what uncertainty, and who can respond? |
| Evaluation & learning | Which criteria trigger revision, who can change the system, and how is improvement tested? |

**Spatial grammar:** Capacity → Operations → Outcomes form the vertical thread; feedback sits left, purpose and governance right, and evaluation below. An arrow is a **question about a relationship**, not proof of causation.

## When to deepen the model

Add a stock-flow diagram for accumulation or resource depletion; a VSM-oriented view for recursive coordination and regulation; a process/capability map for operational boundaries; or a rule/authority view when decision rights matter. Use established enterprise modeling methods when they add necessary precision.

If the enterprise appears capable of doing the right work but repeatedly chooses different behavior, [open the Game Model](/docs/game-model/) to investigate incentives, information and strategic interaction.

## Example: a newsroom

An enterprise model can identify reporting capacity, verification, editorial decisions, platform distribution, audience effects, editorial governance, and the signals used to monitor success. It **does not** establish that engagement metrics *cause* an editorial choice. That causal question requires evidence and may benefit from a nested game model.

## Status

This is a selective, revisable modeling tool; not a complete enterprise architecture standard, a formal causal simulator, or a validated diagnostic instrument. See the [shared methodology](/docs/enterprise-agency-modeling/) and [field guide](/docs/system-canvas-field-guide/).
