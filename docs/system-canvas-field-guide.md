# System Canvas — Practitioner Field Guide

**Provisional method, not yet validated**

## Five moves

**Frame → Map → Explain → Redesign → Test and adapt.**

### Frame

Name the problematic outcome, affected parties, explicit boundary, time horizon, stated purpose, actual operational objectives and available evidence.

### Map: use only the necessary lenses

| Lens | Ask | Draw |
|---|---|---|
| Structural | Who controls resources, depends on whom and crosses boundaries? | Actor/dependency map |
| Behavioral | What recurring actions, incentives and feedback sustain outcomes? | Process or causal loop |
| Epistemic | Who can observe, verify, dispute and correct? | Evidence/information map |
| Deontic | Who decides, must act, enforces and hears appeals? | Rights/obligations matrix |
| Teleological | Which goals are stated, rewarded and contested? | Goal-tension diagram |
| Evaluative | What changes, for whom, and how do we know? | Outcomes and evidence table |

Cross-cutting checks: time, environment, power, strategic spillovers, information, legitimacy.

### Explain

> Because **[rule / dependency / information gap / incentive]**, actors repeatedly **[behavior]**, producing **[outcome]**, reinforced by **[feedback]**.

Tag each causal link: **O** directly observed; **S** source-supported; **H** hypothesis; **U** unknown. Include competing explanations and ways to distinguish them.

### Redesign

> If **[authorized actor]** changes **[lever]**, **[behavior]** should change and **[outcome]** improve because **[mechanism]**.

Compare leverage, feasibility, consent, costs, distributional effects, unintended harms, gaming risks and reversibility. Sometimes better outcomes require redistribution of authority or resources, not refined indicators.

### Test and adapt

Specify baseline, leading measures, outcome indicators, side effects, time window, counterfactual where feasible, stopping rule and who revises the intervention.

### Reflexivity check

Who selected the goal and metric? What does the regulator know? What incentives shape the regulator? Who can audit or replace it? Expand the boundary if these questions materially change the diagnosis.

## Avoid

Do not infer causality from a diagram; treat a stated rule as enforced; equate engagement with welfare; equate perceived credibility with truth; assume common stakeholder goals; treat power as universally zero sum; or report proposed interventions as demonstrated improvements.
