# System Canvas — Practitioner Field Guide

**Enterprise Canvas** is the practitioner interface to [Enterprise & Agency Modeling](/docs/enterprise-agency-modeling/). Its current worksheet emphasizes the [Enterprise Model](/docs/enterprise-model/); use the [Game Model](/docs/game-model/) when agents' choices, rules, information and incentives help explain outcomes. [Human Viable Systems](/docs/human-viable-systems/) provides optional, explicitly normative evaluation criteria.

## Read the system spatially

Use the [interactive spatial diagram](/canvas/) as an **orientation before completing the worksheet**. In the vertical practitioner view, **Capacity → Operations → Outcomes** is the central thread; **Observation & Feedback** sits to the left, **Purpose & Governance** to the right, and **Evaluation & Learning** below. The return paths make revision explicit without implying that learning is automatic.

The arrows are **questions about relationships**, not claims that particular flows or feedback are already verified. Ask where resources accumulate; where they move; what delays intervene; how participants learn of outcomes; who may adjust rules; and whose viability is affected.

### When to reveal another diagram

| If the overview cannot explain... | Reveal... | Check before trusting it |
|---|---|---|
| A resource shortage or bottleneck | Stock/flow, throughput and delay diagram | Units, rates, inventories, delays |
| A persistent pattern or adaptation | Causal-loop or state-transition view | Rival mechanisms and empirical evidence |
| Disputed observation or misinformation | Evidence/provenance map | Sources, corrections, unknowns |
| Inability to change a rule | Institutional authority / IAD action-situation view | Actual rule enforcement and appeal access |
| Conflicting ends | Goal-tension / 4EM view | Distinguish declared goals from incentivized behavior |
| Undesirable side effects | Outcome and impact evaluation view | Counterfactual, affected parties and time horizon |

**Leverage overlay:** when selecting a possible intervention, check stocks and buffers, rates and delays, feedback loops, information flows, rules, and goals. The type of lever does not prove its effect. Record responsible authority, baseline, expected mechanism, side effects and revision criteria.



## The default canvas: minimum viable diagnosis

**Start here before choosing any theoretical lens.** The canvas is a discussion and decision aid, not a complete organizational map.

| Field | Plain-language prompt |
|---|---|
| **Problem** | What undesirable or puzzling outcome keeps recurring? |
| **People and boundary** | Who participates, who is affected, and what lies outside this sketch? |
| **Mechanism** | What rules, dependencies, incentives, information gaps or feedback might reproduce it? |
| **Evidence** | What do we know, what do we infer, and what would disprove the explanation? |
| **Judgment** | For whom is the result good or bad, under which purposes and criteria? |
| **Change** | Who could change which lever, and what consequences should we monitor? |

A novice should be able to explain the sketch in ordinary language. Add detail only when it would change an inference, decision or evaluation.

### Four practical modes

- **Analyze** — establish a sufficiently useful picture. Expand Structure or relevant enterprise-architecture viewpoints only as needed.
- **Diagnose** — explain persistence, show a feedback or strategic mechanism, and write rival hypotheses.
- **Evaluate** — distinguish declared aims, operating metrics, effects, distributional costs, viability and legitimacy; record uncertainty.
- **Refine** — choose an intervention, implementation authority, baseline, safeguards and next observation.

The existing five moves are a **suggested facilitation sequence**, while these modes are **capabilities that can be revisited in any order**.

### When to open a deeper lens

| Symptom | First additional view | Established theory to consult if needed |
|---|---|---|
| Coordination, duplication, brittle dependencies | Structure / Behavior | Enterprise architecture, VSM |
| A rule creates perverse incentives | Authority / Behavior | IAD, institutional economics |
| Disputes over what is true | Knowledge | Evidence evaluation, epistemology |
| Nobody can change an obviously defective rule | Authority / Purpose | IAD, second-order cybernetics |
| Reported success conceals harm to another group | Outcomes / Purpose | Program evaluation, ecological economics |
| Separate institutions reinforce one another's failures | Behavior / Structure | Compositional games, network analysis |

Escalate model complexity **only if the simpler view cannot answer a consequential question**. Stop adding diagrams when the next analytical action is to obtain evidence, consult affected parties, or test a change.

### Usability is not just aesthetics

Review whether independent practitioners can reproduce the reasoning and avoid false causal conclusions. Record **completion time, points of confusion, omissions, unwarranted causal claims, and intervention quality**. A visually elegant canvas that encourages unjustified inference is not accessible in the relevant sense.



## Five moves

**Frame → Map → Explain → Redesign → Test and adapt.**

### Frame

Name the problematic outcome, affected parties, explicit boundary, time horizon, stated purpose, actual operational objectives and available evidence.

### Map: use only the necessary lenses

| Lens | Ask | Draw |
|---|---|---|
| Structural | Who controls resources, depends on whom and crosses boundaries? | Actor/dependency map |
| Behavioral | What recurring actions, incentives and feedback sustain outcomes? | Process or causal loop |
| Epistemic | Who can observe, verify, dispute and correct? | Evidence/information map |
| Deontic | Who decides, must act, enforces and hears appeals? | Rights/obligations matrix |
| Teleological | Which goals are stated, rewarded and contested? | Goal-tension diagram |
| Evaluative | What changes, for whom, and how do we know? | Outcomes and evidence table |

Cross-cutting checks: time, environment, power, strategic spillovers, information, legitimacy.

### Explain

> Because **[rule / dependency / information gap / incentive]**, actors repeatedly **[behavior]**, producing **[outcome]**, reinforced by **[feedback]**.

Tag each causal link: **O** directly observed; **S** source-supported; **H** hypothesis; **U** unknown. Include competing explanations and ways to distinguish them.

### Redesign

> If **[authorized actor]** changes **[lever]**, **[behavior]** should change and **[outcome]** improve because **[mechanism]**.

Compare leverage, feasibility, consent, costs, distributional effects, unintended harms, gaming risks and reversibility. Sometimes better outcomes require redistribution of authority or resources, not refined indicators.

### Test and adapt

Specify baseline, leading measures, outcome indicators, side effects, time window, counterfactual where feasible, stopping rule and who revises the intervention.

### Reflexivity check

Who selected the goal and metric? What does the regulator know? What incentives shape the regulator? Who can audit or replace it? Expand the boundary if these questions materially change the diagnosis.

## Avoid

Do not infer causality from a diagram; treat a stated rule as enforced; equate engagement with welfare; equate perceived credibility with truth; assume common stakeholder goals; treat power as universally zero sum; or report proposed interventions as demonstrated improvements.
