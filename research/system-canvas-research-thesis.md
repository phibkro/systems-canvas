# System Canvas — Research Thesis

## Proposed claim

A structured, reflexive six-lens diagnostic method **may** improve the accuracy, coverage and usefulness of mechanism identification and intervention proposals compared with credible existing approaches. Its value has not yet been demonstrated.

## Hypotheses

| ID | Testable claim | Relevant counterevidence |
|---|---|---|
| H1 | More independently verified, relevant mechanisms identified | More unsupported stories or no gain |
| H2 | Better causal fit, feasibility and safeguards in interventions | Equal or worse blinded review |
| H3 | Reflexive prompts detect additional material governance failures | No difference vs first-order method |
| H4 | Procedure transfers across domains | Extensive domain-specific rewriting needed |
| H5 | Novices perform competently without prior theoretical study | Weak transfer or high instructor dependence |

## Proposed mechanism

Six complementary perspectives prevent analyses from collapsing complex institutional failures into a single lens. Cross-view examination may reveal coupled causes: e.g., platform incentives, newsroom authority, evidence uncertainty and public outcomes. Regulation is itself scrutinized as a system.

## Foundations for expert review

- **General systems theory:** von Bertalanffy, boundaries, open systems and interdependence.
- **Cybernetics:** Wiener and Ashby, feedback and requisite variety.
- **Second-order cybernetics:** von Foerster, the observer's role.
- **Viable System Model:** Stafford Beer, recursive organizational regulation.
- **Applied category theory:** Fong and Spivak, compositional thinking and interfaces, *not* a claimed formal RSML semantics.
- **Compositional game theory:** Ghani, Hedges, Winschel and Zahn, strategically linked open games.
- **Institutional Analysis and Development:** Elinor Ostrom, participants, rules and action situations.
- **4EM/EKD:** goal, process, actor and rule modeling.
- **Epistemic/deontic logic:** rigorous distinctions between belief/knowledge and duty/permission.
- **Causal inference and program evaluation:** identifying effects rather than telling plausible stories.
- **Participatory design and social choice:** whose interests, legitimacy, power, and contested objectives.

These are initial leads, not an exhaustive or verified literature synthesis. A domain expert should challenge the comparisons and supply primary references.

## Essential distinctions

Containment ≠ participation. System ≠ strategic game. Feedback ≠ causal proof. Declared goal ≠ operational incentive ≠ observed function. Norm ≠ enforcement. Authority ≠ legitimacy. Credibility ≠ truth. Viability ≠ ethical desirability. Recursive decomposition ≠ second-order observer inclusion. Power is not generally a finite conserved stock.

## Research workflow

1. Review prior art and find the *incremental* methodological contribution.
2. Operationalize the prompts and examples.
3. Pilot usability and diagnostic reliability.
4. Compare against well-specified alternatives using blinded reviewers.
5. Evaluate actual interventions when ethical and feasible.
6. Publish null findings and refine or narrow the claim.

**Falsification attitude:** if System Canvas increases effort without increasing independently verified quality, simplify or reposition as a teaching aid.
